## 8. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

**Genre:** Journey to the West + Avatar: The Last Airbender, but traveling *east*. With Chrono Trigger's non-linear time structure.

**Inciting Incident:**
Elowen receives a vague "calling" from something she can't identify (the planet's spirit, though she doesn't know this yet). She is given a McGuffin (**The Chronos Keystone**) that opens Timegates. A 1,000-year timeskip/timestop separates her from her era.

**Core Loop:**
- Travel between eras via Timegates (Game 1).
- Meet friends and foes across time.
- Discover the Vorashi threat, the Precursor truth, and her own lineage's role.
- Purify leyline nodes without triggering Ouroboros-Prime.
- Confront the truth about her parents' death (they died during the Vorashi invasion, triggering the Aetheric Implosion).

**Thematic Arc:**
- Elowen doesn't know what she's doing. The calling is vague. She learns through action.
- The aunt knows everything but can't say it.
- Rik anchors her to a home that no longer exists in the form she remembers.
- The 48-minute clock is both a gameplay mechanic and a narrative metaphor: time is a loop, and breaking it requires understanding *why* it loops.

**The 1,000-Year Timeskip:**
- Elowen was in temporal stasis for 1,000 years. Subjective experience: ~2 years.
- She emerges to find: monarchy dissolved, Conclave in power, tech adopted, Vorashi scarred the continent, Rik is now older than her, and she's been presumed dead/forgotten.
- This is the emotional core of Game 1's opening.
